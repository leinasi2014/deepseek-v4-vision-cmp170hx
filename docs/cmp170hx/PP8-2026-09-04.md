|---|---|

## Measured results (SAT harness: ~2k-token prompt × 1024 forced tokens, N concurrent HTTP clients)

| Concurrency | Aggregate tok/s | Per-request tok/s |
|---|---|---|
| single stream (2k+256) | 75.1 | 75.1 |
| 16 | 198–368* | 13–24 |
| 64 | **672.6** | 10.7 |
| 128 | **660.0** | 5.2 |

\* short-window runs; the saturating numbers are the 64/128 rows.
VRAM peak 58.9 GiB/card (62 GiB red line respected), Xid = 0 across every run.

Scaling is compute-bound as expected: per-step time grows ~22 ms (batch 1) →
~130 ms (batch 64); per-request throughput falls as the batch shares the step.

## DSpark on/off at high concurrency (64c, bat8192): 514 vs 478 — wash

Speculative decoding is NOT harmful at high batch on this stack; keep it for
the single-stream latency win (75 vs ~55 tok/s).

## A/B vs the genuine old pp8 stack (same harness, same day)

| Arm | 64c | 128c |
|---|---|---|
| Vision-Exp + new engine (overlay7) | 514 → **672** (final shape) | 584 → **660** |
| DSV4-FINAL (text) + old `dsv4-a100:tip` engine | 333 | 368 |

The new engine + vision weights beat the genuine old combination by **~55–100%**
under identical measurement.

## Correction: the historical "1219 tok/s" baseline was a misread

The 08-31 old-pp8 engine logs show, per 10-second metrics window:

```
Accepted: 1219 tokens, Drafted: 1630 tokens
Avg generation throughput: 152.9 tokens/s, Running: 2 reqs
```

"1219" is the **accepted-draft-token COUNT per 10 s window** (= 121.9 tok/s
accepted throughput), not a serving throughput. The real old-pp8 operating
point was **~155 tok/s aggregate at 2 concurrent** (acceptance 75%, mean
acceptance length 4.7). The derived "3.16B tokens/month capacity" figure
inherits the same 10× error (~0.3B at the true rate under that load).

Against the *actual* old level, today's PP8 profile delivers ~4.3× the
aggregate throughput at 64 concurrent.
